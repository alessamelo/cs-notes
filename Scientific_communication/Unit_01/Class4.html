Characteristics of Research

Research is Systematic

Research is systematic because it follows a sequence of steps. This allows the research process to be documented and reproduced.

Research is Methodological

Research follows established methods and procedures to collect, analyze, and interpret information.

Types of Research Studies

1. Exploratory Study

An exploratory study is a small-scale study that examines a limited selection of a phenomenon or problem.

Common Instruments

  • Focus groups
  • Interviews
  • Surveys
  • Observation

2. Descriptive Study

Descriptive studies are mainly based on observation and describe the characteristics of a phenomenon or population.

One possible weakness is that descriptive studies may be considered less impactful or more difficult to publish.

3. Correlational Study

A correlational study examines the relationship between two or more variables or events.

  • Independent variables
  • Dependent variables

Two events can be correlated, meaning that changes in one variable are associated with changes in another.

Important: Correlation does not necessarily imply causation.

4. Explanatory Study

An explanatory study attempts to explain a topic or phenomenon in greater detail.

These studies can serve as a preamble to a larger investigation. Their results may not be conclusive, but they can provide new perspectives on the phenomenon being studied.

Research questions can be generated from explanatory studies.

5. Quasi-Experimental Study

A quasi-experimental study is characterized by the fact that the study subjects are not randomly selected or randomly assigned to groups. Instead, the subjects or groups may already exist or have been established previously.

This type of research can use both qualitative and quantitative data.

6. Experimental Study

An experimental investigation is carried out using a scientific approach in which one or more variables are manipulated while other variables are controlled or kept constant.

The researcher measures the effect of the manipulated variable on the dependent variable.

An experimental investigation can be conducted in a laboratory or another controlled environment.

An experiment is considered successful when a change in the dependent variable can be attributed to the manipulation of the independent variable under the experimental conditions.

Research Paradigms

Qualitative Research

  • Associated with phenomenology
  • Greater researcher interaction or intervention
  • Subjective perspective
  • Focuses on deep and contextual understanding
  • Studies the experiences and meanings associated with a phenomenon

Quantitative Research

  • Associated with positivism
  • Uses measurable data
  • Emphasizes replicability
  • Maintains greater distance between the researcher and the phenomenon
  • Uses statistical analysis

Organization of Research

  1. Define the topic
  2. Search for information
  3. Analyze the data
  4. Formulate the problem
  5. Operationalize the problem
  6. Evaluate the feasibility of the investigation

Boolean Method

The Boolean method is used to obtain more precise search results for a specific topic.

Descriptors

The first step is to determine the descriptors, which can represent fixed and variable terms related to the research topic.

Boolean logic is based on a binary system, represented by 0 and 1.

Number of Combinations

If there are n descriptors, the number of possible binary combinations is:

2n

Boolean operators such as AND, OR, and NOT can be used to construct and test different research search equations.

Boolean Operators

  • AND: Requires both terms to be present.
  • OR: Allows either term to be present.
  • NOT: Excludes a specific term from the search.

Formulating the Research Problem

1. Define the Problem

Identify and discuss a specific topic or problem that will be investigated.

2. Delimit the Problem

Establish the boundaries of the research problem by specifying what aspects will and will not be studied.

Research Feasibility

Before beginning an investigation, it is necessary to determine whether the research is feasible.

Factors to Consider

  • Available sources
  • Available resources
  • Available time and schedule
