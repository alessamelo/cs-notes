1. Title

The title should communicate the main content of the article and attract the reader's attention.

A good title should be:

  • Brief
  • Accurate
  • Specific
  • Clear about the topic of the research
  • Include relevant keywords for search and indexing
Important: When appropriate, the title should mention the model or technique used in the research so that the methodology is immediately clear.
Example:

Semidescentralized Federated Learning to Analyze the Accuracy of Fatalities in Ecuador

2. Abstract

The abstract summarizes the main content of the scientific article.

It should provide a concise overview of the research and allow the reader to understand the general purpose and content of the article.

Keywords

The abstract is generally accompanied by a set of keywords related to the main concepts of the article.

Graphical Abstract

Some journals also request a graphical abstract.

A graphical abstract is an image that summarizes the main content or methodology of the article visually.

It can represent the research as a pipeline, showing the main stages of the proposed approach.

3. Introduction

The introduction presents the research problem and establishes the context for the study.

Contributions

The contributions section explains what the article contributes to the research area.

The contributions should make clear what is added by the proposed research.

4. Related Work

Related Work presents the state of the art and explains how previous scientific studies approach the same or related problem.

For each relevant scientific article reviewed, explain aspects such as:

  • The methodology used
  • The algorithms implemented
  • The techniques employed
  • The main results
  • The contribution of the study
  • The limitations or areas that could still be improved
Recommended structure for each paper:

Methodology → Algorithm/Technique → Results → Contribution → Limitations
Recommendation: Avoid being excessively drastic when describing the limitations of previous research. Limitations should be presented objectively and constructively.

5. Scientific Articles for the State of the Art

To understand the state of the art, it is important to review appropriate scientific literature rather than relying only on general sources.

The reviewed studies should be relevant to the research topic and should allow comparison between different methodologies, algorithms, techniques, and results.

Surveys

Survey articles are scientific review papers that analyze and organize existing research around a particular topic.

Current surveys can serve as bibliographic review articles and are useful for compacting and organizing information from a larger body of research.

Example: A survey about GANs can review and organize multiple approaches, algorithms, applications, and findings related to Generative Adversarial Networks.

6. H-Index and Researchers

The h-index is an indicator related to a researcher's scientific impact and is associated with the number of citations received by their publications.

Researchers can have different h-index values depending on their publication and citation history.

Researchers who work extensively in a particular field may also publish survey articles that organize the existing research in that area.

7. Organizing the State of the Art

A common way to organize the reviewed literature is through a comparison table.

Article Methodology Algorithm / Technique Results Contribution Limitations
Paper 1 Method used Algorithm / technique Main results Main contribution Areas for improvement
Paper 2 Method used Algorithm / technique Main results Main contribution Areas for improvement

This makes it easier to compare different scientific approaches and identify gaps that can motivate the proposed research.

8. Methodology

The Methodology section describes how the research was conducted and how the proposed approach was implemented.

The methodology should provide enough information to explain the research process and the techniques used.

Publication consideration: The methodology contains important technical details of the proposed work. Care should be taken when sharing unpublished methodological details before the work has been submitted or approved for publication.
